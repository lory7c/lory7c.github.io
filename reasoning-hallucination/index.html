A wrong answer is not a diagnosis. UIPCA distinguishes unsupported premises, invalid inferences, dependent reuse of a confirmed error before repair, visible answer-trace consistency, and action-policy failure. These are independent diagnostic questions, not five stages. Visible consistency does not establish hidden reasoning.

Causal evidence requires a specified intervention, matched conditions, separate reasoning and factuality measures, thresholds, and uncertainty. Reviewed comparisons do not establish the joint adverse effect under that criterion. Unestablished does not mean harmless.

Full, summary, and hidden interfaces expose different evidence. Unknown is not absent. Evidence can support a repair; unresolved support may instead require abstention. Release and persistent writes require separate checks for evidence, authority, and provenance.

Abstract

Reasoning errors can propagate into later decisions and memory. This survey synthesizes 312 papers and first-party reports on text-based reasoning hallucinations around three questions: what evidence is observable, what study designs establish, and which corrective actions the evidence supports.

UIPCA records unsupported premises (U), invalid inferences (I), dependent reuse (P), visible answer-trace consistency (C), and action-policy failures (A). Across 58 reviewed sources, no comparison establishes that a specified intervention improves reasoning while reducing factual reliability under matched conditions.

The synthesis connects diagnosis to verification, repair, selective release, and persistent-state control across memory, tools, and training feedback.

Interface Visibility

A visible rationale is generated text. Its consistency and its causal role are different questions.

Full trace

Ordered steps permit local support checks, inference checks, and inspection of visible dependencies.

Visible structure does not establish the mechanism of hidden computation.

Summary

Disclosed claims can be checked against the task and its evidence, with a partial view of the process.

Omitted steps remain unobserved; they cannot be marked error-free.

Hidden reasoning

The prompt, designated evidence, answer, and observed action support output and policy checks.

Unavailable process diagnostics remain unknown, rather than zero.

Analysis

The survey links process diagnosis, causal evidence, and decisions about release and persistent state.

UIPCA Diagnosis

Different failures need different repairs

Unsupported premises, invalid inferences, dependent reuse, visible answer-trace consistency, and action-policy failures identify distinct repair points.

Mechanism Hypotheses

Separate observations from causal tests

Five falsifiable hypotheses connect the reviewed evidence to proposed experiments. Matched interventions are needed to attribute a joint change in reasoning and factuality.

Persistent-State Control

An answer can become tomorrow's evidence

Release and write decisions require different checks. Memory, tool state, and training feedback also need evidence binding, authority checks, versioning, and downstream recovery.

UIPCA records confirmed failures (1), ruled-out failures (0), or insufficient evidence (unknown). Repetition alone does not establish dependent reuse, and consistency alone does not establish factuality.

Detection and Release Control

Verification and repair inform release. State-changing actions also require evidence, authority, and version checks.

Release

Evaluate the whole cascade

Report selective risk together with coverage, repair success, review workload, and cost.

Write

Bind evidence to state changes

Preserve unknowns, check write authority and versions, and record provenance.

Recover

Follow affected consumers

Correcting a stored record does not by itself repair derived summaries, actions, or feedback.
